# PRD 00 — FFL Pilot Mandate

**Status:** V0 scope authority

## Objective

Prove that one FFL operating team can run one managed farm and crop season with more disciplined execution and faster learning than a collection of calls, spreadsheets, and memory.

The pilot is an operating-system test, not a promise that software alone removes agronomic or weather risk.

## The first operating loop

The initial loop is intentionally narrow:

1. A manager creates the active farm, blocks, season, and crop plan.
2. An agronomist defines the handful of stage-critical checkpoints and required evidence.
3. A field operator receives and completes work or reports a deviation.
4. A farm manager or agronomist owns every material deviation.
5. The team records what decision it made, why, and what happened at the next checkpoint.
6. Leadership sees only the decisions, risks, and blocked work that require attention.

## Pilot users and their promises

| User | Promise FFL makes |
|---|---|
| Field operator | “You always know today’s work, the minimum proof required, and where to flag something abnormal.” |
| Farm manager | “You can see what is incomplete, blocked, overdue, or materially different from plan without chasing multiple people.” |
| Agronomist | “Your protocol is translated into crop-stage work and you see the exceptions that require your judgment.” |
| Leadership | “You receive a trustworthy operating picture, not a vanity dashboard.” |

## In scope

- One managed farm with any number of blocks.
- One active season and a configurable crop plan.
- Named team members and roles.
- Planned work, field signals, exceptions, decisions, evidence, and outcome reviews.
- Soil baseline and improvement checkpoints as operational context.
- A short daily operating view and a weekly learning review.

## Out of scope

- A farmer marketplace, financing product, or generic CRM.
- Automating crop selection or making weather-dependent decisions without human approval.
- Full accounting, payroll, contracting, procurement, or downstream buyer workflow.
- A mobile application for every external farmer before field-team adoption is proven.
- Mass historical data migration before the pilot can run natively.

## Pilot operating cadence

### Before the season

- Establish the farm/block context, season, crop plan, starting soil state, crop-stage calendar, and accountable people.
- Define the first operating playbook and the evidence required at each checkpoint.
- Hold a readiness review where every critical workstream has an owner and every known uncertainty is explicit.

### During the season

- Field operators use a daily work list and an exception form.
- Managers run a daily exception/ownership review and a weekly plan/re-plan review.
- Agronomists approve or amend material interventions and log the hypothesis or rationale.
- Leadership sees exceptions and decisions, not raw activity volume as a proxy for progress.

### At the season boundary

- Conduct a structured review of operating discipline, validated practices, failed assumptions, unresolved questions, and the next version of each playbook.
- Preserve all material evidence and decisions so the second farm or season begins with an institutional memory.

## V0 quality bar

- No person needs to ask “which version of the plan are we following?”
- No material exception lacks a named owner and next action.
- No closed material action lacks either evidence or an explicit reason it could not be collected.
- A new team member can understand the current state of a block from the product without an oral briefing.
- The product remains useful when an external data integration is unavailable.

## Pilot measurement contract

Before pilot go-live, the operations lead configures and publishes:

- Three to five material exception classes, each with severity definitions, primary owner, fallback supervisor, response target, and required completion evidence.
- The stage-critical work categories, due-window rules, and what counts as on-time completion.
- The baseline, target, calculation, review cadence, and accountable owner for on-time critical work, time-to-owner, time-to-response, evidence completeness, decision-outcome review, and active field adoption.
- The weekly operating review and the person who records its decisions and proposed playbook changes.

The product does not fabricate a baseline. Missing or insufficient baseline data is shown explicitly and becomes a pilot-learning gap.

## Success measures

The pilot is successful when the team can show operational improvement, not when it simply generates more records:

- Percentage of stage-critical work completed on time with required evidence.
- Time from material exception to accountable owner and documented response.
- Percentage of material decisions with a later outcome review.
- Number of validated playbook changes carried into the next season.
- User-reported reduction in time spent chasing status or reconstructing history.

## Open implementation constraint

The first crop, exact acreage, and farm arrangement are configuration. V0 must not embed them in code, form labels, or reporting logic.
