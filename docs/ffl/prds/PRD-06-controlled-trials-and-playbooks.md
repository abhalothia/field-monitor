# PRD 06 — Controlled Trials and Playbook Validation

**Status:** V1/V2, when FFL has a stable operating loop

## Objective

Give FFL a disciplined way to test meaningful operating ideas—crop practice, timing, soil treatment, irrigation approach, labour process, or technology—without overstating causality or losing the context needed to reuse a result.

## Product principle

An experiment is a governed operational protocol, not an informal note that a new idea was tried. The product should help FFL learn faster while keeping safety, crop quality, and farmer/landowner commitments above experimental ambition.

## Required model

| Record | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Hypothesis | The expected effect, scope, decision it informs, and named owner. |
| Eligibility rule | Which season crop allocations may participate and why. |
| Protocol version | Treatment, comparator, work instructions, measurement schedule, and evidence requirements. |
| Guardrail | Safety, quality, compliance, cash, or operational threshold that pauses or stops the trial. |
| Confounder | Material condition that may invalidate comparison: weather, soil baseline, irrigation, labour disruption, pest event, or timing variance. |
| Conclusion | Result, confidence level, evidence, reviewer, and playbook decision. |

## Requirements

- A trial has a named hypothesis, owner, eligible allocations, treatment/comparator, protocol version, start/end conditions, and explicit decision it is meant to inform.
- The protocol identifies outcome measurements, measurement method, cadence, and required confounder records before work starts.
- A manager can pause or stop a trial when a guardrail is crossed; the reason and all pending work are retained.
- The product prevents a draft conclusion from becoming a standard playbook without review by the assigned accountable agronomist or operations lead.
- Conclusions state the confidence level and limitations. They do not claim that a result is causal when important comparison context is missing.
- A WhatsApp prompt may distribute an already-approved trial work instruction or collect a candidate observation from an opted-in participant, but the event identifies its protocol version and cannot enroll, randomize, alter, pause, or conclude a trial.

## Success criteria

- A reviewer can reconstruct the tested protocol, participating allocations, deviations, confounders, evidence, and conclusion.
- A stopped trial remains an auditable learning record rather than disappearing.
- A promoted playbook points to the specific trial evidence and its limitations.

## Non-goals

- Statistical certainty from a single small pilot.
- Running trials that bypass food safety, legal, commercial, or agronomic approval.
- Automated experiment selection or autonomous protocol changes.
